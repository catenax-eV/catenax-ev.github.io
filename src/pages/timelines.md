# Release Timelines

Welcome to the **Release Timelines** overview page.
Here you can always find the **most up-to-date release schedules** for **Catena-X ecosystem release**.

For a detailed explanation of the overall process and the release cycle, please refer to the [Release management](/release-management) section.

## Overview of further upcoming releases

The following overview provides a high-level roadmap of the planned Catena-X ecosystem releases. Each ecosystem release consists of two release cycles.
The overview illustrates the continuous evolution of the ecosystem through alternating major and minor releases, ensuring a balanced approach between innovation and consolidation.
Each major release establishes the foundation for new developments and strategic enhancements, while the subsequent minor release focuses on refinement, stabilization, and the continuous improvement of the introduced capabilities. This predictable release cadence supports transparent planning and enables all participating stakeholders to align their activities and contributions accordingly.

## Catena-X and Eclipse Tractus-X Release Schedules

The following sections describe the **release cycles**, including both **Catena-X internal milestones** and **Eclipse Tractus-X open milestones**.
Each phase contributes to transparency, alignment, and coordinated delivery across all participating committees and expert groups.

:::info

- Milestones colored in green or marked with the **Tractus-X** logo are **open to the public** and available on the [Tractus-X Open Meetings page](https://eclipse-tractusx.github.io/community/open-meetings).
- Milestones colored in orange or marked with the Catena-X logo represent internal sessions organized by the **Catena-X Association**.

:::

### Catena-X Proteus, incl. Eclipse Tractus-X Release 27.03

Following the major release CX-Neptune, CX-Proteus is directly connected to its parent release, as Proteus is one of Neptune’s moons.
This minor release therefore represents continuity and close evolution within the established ecosystem rather than a change of direction.
It focuses on strengthening and refining what was introduced with CX-Neptune, ensuring stability, coherence, and improved interoperability across the ecosystem.
In this sense, CX-Proteus acts as a natural extension of its major release, orbiting around its core innovations and gradually maturing them.

:::info

Starting with the **Catena-X 27.03 release**, the standards release process will follow a more flexible and continuous approach.
As part of the introduction of this new release process with the goal of achieving **operational excellence**, the **26.12 release was skipped**. As a result, **Proteus consists of only one release part, 27.03**.

Instead of aligning standard development with fixed release phases, **standards can now be developed continuously throughout the release cycle**. This allows Expert Groups and contributors to work independently of predefined development windows while fostering ongoing collaboration and iterative improvements.

To ensure a coordinated and high-quality release, **a dedicated submission window remains in place**. Within this timeframe, completed standards can be submitted and will progress through the established review and approval process, including the required quality, technical, and governance checks before becoming part of the official release.

This updated process provides greater flexibility for standard development while maintaining a structured and transparent validation process, ensuring that all published standards meet the expected quality and interoperability requirements of the Catena-X ecosystem.

For more information, see the explained release phases in the chapter [Timeline – Phases and Milestones in Detail](#timeline--phases-and-milestones-in-detail).

:::

## Timeline – Phases and Milestones in Detail

The following section provides a detailed description of every phase and milestone shown in the release timeline.
The timeline is structured into three overarching sections - **Preparation**, **Standard Review & TX Development**, and **Release Closing** - and runs in parallel for **Catena-X** and **Eclipse Tractus-X**.

---

## Catena-X

### Preparation

The Preparation section covers the activities that take place **after the standard has already been fully written** by the responsible Expert Group or Committee. It comprises the **Briefing Release Planning**, the **Internal Planning Day**, and the **Expert Feedback Phase**.

During this phase, Expert Groups and Committees **present their completed standards or work** to the wider community and collect **final feedback** before the formal review process begins. The Preparation section therefore ensures that all standard candidates are aligned, transparent, and ready to enter the Standard Review process.

---

#### Briefing Release Planning

The Briefing Release Planning is a dedicated **meeting** that specifically prepares all stakeholders for the upcoming **[Internal Planning Day](#internal-planning-day)**.
Objectives of the meeting:

- **Preparing participants for the Internal Planning Day**, ensuring that everyone knows which content to bring and how the sessions will be structured.
- Sharing relevant **organizational updates and contextual information**.
- Explaining the **timeline, milestones, and expectations** of the upcoming release cycle.

This ensures that the Internal Planning Day can be used efficiently and that all participants enter the meeting with a shared understanding.

---

#### Internal Planning Day

The Internal Planning Day is the **central planning meeting** of the release cycle on the Catena-X side. Its purpose is to formally define **which completed standards will be included in the upcoming release**.

Prerequisite for participation is that each standard candidate is **final and ready for review** at this point in time. "Final" means that the standard is **fully drafted and written** and can be handed over for the subsequent review process without any content gaps.

**Content of the meeting:**

- **Presentation of the issue for each finalized standard** planned for inclusion in the upcoming release. The issue provides an overview of the standard's scope, objectives, and relevant dependencies, and serves as the central place where the corresponding pull request (PR) for the finalized standard is linked and tracked.
- **Collaborative discussion and alignment** of the presented content to ensure transparency, alignment, and a shared understanding across all stakeholders.

:::info

After the Internal Planning Day, **no new standards may be added** to the release. From this point on, only feedback on already-presented standards can be incorporated. Standards that were not presented during the Internal Planning Day will **not be part of the release**.

:::  

---

#### Expert Feedback Phase *(2 weeks)*

During the Expert Feedback Phase, the responsible **Expert Group** takes the lead and uses the time to collect and incorporate final feedback on the already completed standard. Key responsibilities include:

- Checking all **dependencies** of the standard (It is recommended to use the [dependency graph](https://catenax-ev.github.io/standards-graph) here as well).
- Addressing **concerns and input** from other Expert Groups and stakeholders.
- Pushing **content changes** into the standard based on the received feedback.

This is the last phase in which the Expert Group has **full ownership over content decisions**. Until the Completeness Check begins, the Expert Group decides autonomously whether to incorporate feedback or not.

---

### Standard Review & TX Development

The Standard Review & Eclipse Tractus-X Development phase runs in parallel for Catena-X and Eclipse Tractus-X, with each initiative focusing on different aspects of the release process.

For Catena-X, this phase is dedicated to the review and validation of standard candidates. In parallel, the Eclipse Tractus-X community continues the development, implementation, and integration of the corresponding open-source components.
For details on the activities taking place within Eclipse Tractus-X during this phase, please refer to the [Eclipse Tractus-X](#eclipse-tractus-x) section.

---

#### Standard Application Window

The Standard Application Window is the overarching timeframe covering all formal review, quality assurance, and Opt-Out activities of the release cycle. Within this window, standards are validated, reviewed by experts, and prepared for their transition into official Catena-X standards.

---

#### Quality Assurance Phase

The Quality Assurance Phase represents the **central review and validation stage** of the Catena-X release cycle. With the start of this phase, the standard owners formally hand over their completed standards to the **TC4S (Technical Committee for Standardization)**, which assumes coordination of the entire review process up to the final release decision.

From this point on, all standard candidates enter a **structured review and validation process**. Feedback can still be incorporated, but only in **close alignment with TC4S and the assigned reviewing experts**.

The objective of this phase is to ensure that every standard candidate is **complete, consistent, interoperable, and aligned with the quality expectations** of the Catena-X ecosystem. To achieve this, TC4S orchestrates a structured review involving the responsible committees and domain experts, each contributing their specific expertise to the evaluation.

At the end of the phase, the **overarching committee formally confirms** that the standard fulfills the expectations defined by the responsible Expert Group and is ready to proceed to the next stage of the release process.

:::info

With the start of the Quality Assurance Phase, all review feedback becomes **normative**. Mandatory findings must be resolved before a standard can proceed. Standard candidates with unresolved mandatory findings are considered **not release-ready** and will be excluded from the release.

:::  

---

##### Quality Gate 1

**Quality Gate 1** marks the first formal checkpoint within the Quality Assurance Phase. Its purpose is to ensure that only standard candidates aligned with the **values, goals, and quality principles** of the Catena-X data ecosystem proceed further in the release process. Each standard is systematically evaluated against defined criteria, focusing on **completeness, relevance, redundancy, and conformance**.

Two types of evaluation criteria apply:

- **Hard criteria:** Non-compliance leads to the **immediate exclusion** of the standard from the release.  
- **Soft criteria:** Non-compliance does not result in exclusion. The responsible Expert Group is given the **opportunity to address the identified issues** during the ongoing Quality Assurance Phase in order to remain part of the release.

At the conclusion of Quality Gate 1, all Expert Groups are **formally notified** whether their standard has been accepted for the release — including a transparent explanation of the underlying reasons.  

---

#### Quality Gate 2

The second Quality Gate ("Quality Gate 2") ensures that only standard candidates that correspond to the quality expectations of Catena-X are being released. During Quality Gate 2, the **maturity of the standard is verified** and — provided a green light is given — a **technical recommendation for its release** is made to the Executive Board.

This milestone marks the formal transition from the Quality Assurance Phase to the Opt-Out preparation activities.

---

#### Prep Opt-Out *(1 week)*

A short preparation phase to get all standards and communication ready before the official Opt-Out phase begins. Standards are finalized for external visibility, and stakeholders are prepared for the decision-making window.

---

#### Start Opt-Out

After the formal approval, an Opt-Out period of **45 days** is required, during which all association members are presented with the standard candidates ahead of their official release to the public. After the 45-day period has passed, the Executive Board is presented the standard candidates again for a **final approval for publication**, marking their official transition from being a standard candidate into an **official Catena-X standard**.

---

#### Opt-Out Town Hall

A dedicated **meeting** held during the Opt-Out phase to **present the standard candidates with the community**. The Town Hall provides transparency on the standards up for approval and offers members the opportunity to ask questions.

---

### Release Closing

The Release Closing section marks the finalization and publication of the release. It ensures that all approved standards and outputs are complete, verified, and properly documented for both internal and external stakeholders - providing full transparency, traceability, and quality assurance.

---

#### Release Finalization *(1.5 weeks)*

The final phase before publication. During this phase, all necessary activities to ensure a smooth and transparent release are carried out:

- All approved standards are **consolidated into the release package**.
- Detailed **Release Notes** are created.

---

#### Publish Release

This milestone marks the **official publication of the Catena-X release**. All approved standards and normative documents become publicly available and officially valid within the Catena-X ecosystem.

---

#### Recap Release

The Recap Release is a dedicated **meeting** that provides a comprehensive **presentation of the release outcomes**. It also provides an **outlook and vision for upcoming releases**.

---

## Eclipse Tractus-X

The Eclipse Tractus-X section of the release cycle is initiated by the **Open Planning Day**, which serves as the **common starting point** for both the FOSS development and the KIT development activities. From this shared kick-off, both streams evolve in parallel and ultimately merge into a joint release finalization and publication.

### Open Planning Day

The Open Planning Day is an **open Tractus-X meeting** and marks the **official start of the Tractus-X activities** within the release cycle. During this session, product teams present their planned features and discuss the feasibility of implementation, enabling a shared understanding of scope, dependencies, and technical challenges across both the FOSS and KIT streams.

It provides the foundation for the subsequent development phases by aligning all contributors on the release scope and expectations.

Further information can be found in the [official documentation](https://eclipse-tractusx.github.io/docs/oss/release-process#open-planning-day).
Details on how to join are available here: [Open Planning Day](https://eclipse-tractusx.github.io/community/open-meetings/#one-time-meetings).

---

### FOSS (Free and Open-Source Software)

The FOSS section comprises the **development of the software** in the context of Tractus-X. These components are implemented by dedicated product teams, each responsible for specific functional units. Once development is complete, the section concludes with a final integration and a comprehensive end-to-end testing process to ensure system readiness.

---

#### Development Phase

During this phase, teams are allocated dedicated time to **develop and contribute to FOSS components** that are part of the overall ecosystem. Product teams work on their assigned components, coordinate with other teams on dependencies, and prepare their deliverables for the upcoming testing phase.

---

#### Opt-In/Opt-Out of the Test Phase

This milestone marks the point at which all teams intending to participate in the upcoming release must **create their Release Check Issue**. By doing so, they formally indicate their **commitment to contribute to the release** and **provide initial information** on their **planned deliverables**.

---

#### Briefing Testing

The Briefing Testing is an **open Tractus-X meeting** that provides **detailed guidance** on roles, responsibilities, and deadlines for the testing phase, ensuring that everyone involved understands their tasks and deliverables.

Details and access can be found here: [Briefing Testing](https://eclipse-tractusx.github.io/community/open-meetings/#one-time-meetings).

---

#### Feature Freeze

The Feature Freeze marks a milestone in the release process, at which point **no further development** of new features or functionality is permitted for this release. This ensures system stability and allows teams to focus exclusively on integration, testing, and defect resolution in preparation for the release.

---

#### INT Deployment

In this phase, the release candidates are **deployed on the INT (Integration) environment**. This provides the technical basis for the subsequent end-to-end testing across all components.

---

#### Kick-off Testing

The Kick-off Testing is an **open Tractus-X meeting** held at the start of the E2E test phase in the regular daily slot at **8:30 – 9:00 a.m.** It officially marks the beginning of the coordinated testing activities across all product teams.

Details and access can be found here: [Kick-off Testing](https://eclipse-tractusx.github.io/community/open-meetings/#one-time-meetings).

---

#### Testing Phase

In this phase, tests are executed and status is reported consistently by the product teams in **daily meetings from 8:30 – 9:00 a.m. CET**. The phase ensures that all components work seamlessly together and that any remaining defects are identified and resolved before publication.

---

### KITs (Keep It Together)

KITs are available to everyone as part of the Eclipse Tractus-X open-source project, which facilitates participation in the **design and development of KITs**. The KIT section comprises the development, review, and finalization of the KITs in the context of Tractus-X.

---

#### Development Phase (KITs)

During this phase, KIT contributors work on the **creation and refinement of their KITs**. This includes preparing content, aligning with dependent components, and coordinating with reviewers to ensure that the KITs meet the quality expectations of the Tractus-X community.

---

#### KIT Review

The KIT Review phase ensures that all submitted KITs undergo a **structured technical and editorial review**. Reviewers verify content quality, consistency, and alignment with the overall Tractus-X release before the KITs are officially merged and published.

---

#### KIT Initial PR

The KIT Initial PR marks the point at which KIT creators **prepare and submit an initial pull request** for their KIT. The initial PR enables **reviewers to start their review activities early**, allowing feedback to be gathered and addressed in a timely manner. This ensures a smoother review process and better alignment with the open-source contribution standards of Eclipse Tractus-X.  

---

#### KIT final PR

The KIT Final PR marks the **deadline by which the finalized pull request must be submitted**. Only KITs whose final PR is available by this milestone can be **guaranteed to be included in the upcoming release**, as reviewers need sufficient time to complete their review before the release closing.

KITs submitted **after this deadline** cannot be ensured to make it into the release and may be deferred to a later cycle.

---

### Release Closing (Tractus-X)

The Release Closing section for Tractus-X marks the finalization and publication of all FOSS components and KITs. It ensures that all outputs are complete, verified, and properly documented, providing confidence to all participating teams and external partners that the release meets the agreed quality standards.

---

#### Release Finalization

During this phase, all necessary activities to ensure a smooth and transparent release are carried out. For Tractus-X, this includes the preparation of the **changelog** and a **review of the Release Check Issues**. Any outstanding questions are clarified directly with the respective product teams.

---

#### Publish Release

This milestone marks the **official publication of the Eclipse Tractus-X release** — released in parallel to the Catena-X release. All FOSS components and KITs become publicly available for the ecosystem.

---

#### Recap Release

The Recap Release provides a comprehensive **presentation of the release outcomes**, including a live system demonstration to showcase the functional and technical progress achieved. It also serves as a joint retrospective and outlook for the next cycle.
