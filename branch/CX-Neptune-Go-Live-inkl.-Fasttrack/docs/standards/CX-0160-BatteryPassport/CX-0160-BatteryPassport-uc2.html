CX-0160-2 Supplier Data Management: Provisioning of Battery Passport Relevant Data by Suppliers v.1.0.0

[!IMPORTANT] Draft Standard

This version of CX-0160-2 is published as a preview. It describes the intended scope, the submodels and the role-based data obligations for supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. data provisioning, so that the concept can be reviewed and discussed by the relevant expert groups and implementation partners. Certification against this standard is not possible as of this release, as substantial changes are expected in the subsequent release.

Harmonisation with the base battery passport models (joint IDTA/Catena-X model) and the delimitation to CX-0160-1 are still under discussion, and feedback from the ongoing review is still being worked in. An updated version of this standard is targeted for a subsequent release.

ABSTRACT​

This standard is an extension to the CX-0160 Battery Passport Data Management standards. It is part of a series for multiple use cases, covering Use Case (2): Provisioning of Battery Passport Relevant Data by SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.. The standard applies to suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. that provide products to battery manufacturers, including but not limited to:

  • Material SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.
  • Component SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.
  • Cell Producers

SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. covered by this standard are not responsible for publishing a Battery Passport or Digital Product Passport (DPP). The purpose of this standard is to ensure that battery producers receive harmonized, machine-readable, and interoperable data contributions that can be incorporated into Battery Passport generation processes. The standard specifies roles and responsibilities, digital twins and semantic models, mandatory and optional information requirements by supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. role, and APIsAPI An API is a way for two or more computer programs to communicate with each other. and processes required for the data provider to share battery instance and battery type data with the data consumer.

FOR WHOM IS THE STANDARD DESIGNED​

This standard is intended for organizations supplying materials, components, or cells to battery manufacturers and for organizations receiving such information to create the Battery Passport.

Data Providers​

Examples include:

  • Material SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.
  • Component SuppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.
  • Cell Producers

Data Consumers​

Examples include:

  • Battery Manufacturers
  • battery manufacturers acting as Economic Operators under the EU Battery Regulation
  • Tier-1 suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. collecting data from material producers or other upstream suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.

1 INTRODUCTION​

Battery Passports require information originating from multiple actors throughout the battery value chain. While battery manufacturers remain responsible for creating and publishing Battery Passports, a substantial share of the underlying information originates from upstream suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.. This standard establishes a mechanism for exchanging supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.-generated information through Catena-X Digital Twins and standardized semantic models.

The objective is to:

  • Create a common understanding of which information is needed throughout the value chain to create Battery Passports
  • Define standardized data models to reduce complexity, redundancy and facilitate implementation throughout the whole value chain
  • Reduce manual reporting efforts
  • Improve interoperability across the value chain
  • Protecting business confidentiality via controlled data access
  • Support battery manufacturers to publish DPPs
  • Enable traceable sustainability and product information exchange

This standard does not transfer regulatory responsibility from battery manufacturers to suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.. Instead, it defines a common framework through which suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. can provide the information required by battery manufacturers to fulfil their obligations.

1.1 AUDIENCE & SCOPE​

This section is non-normative

This standard is relevant for the following Catena-X roles:

Data Provider​

Data Providers are actors in the battery value chain that supply Battery Passport-relevant information to other actors. A Data Provider may be any organization that holds and shares relevant data, regardless of its position in the value chain. It should be noted that the battery production supply chain may involve a wider range of supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. types than the three categories described above.

Data Consumer​

Data Consumers are actors in the battery value chain that request, receive, and use supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. information for Battery Passport creation, regulatory compliance, or any organization that requires Battery Passport-relevant data from another actor in the value chain.

This standard specifies how supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.-related Battery Passport information is modeled, provisioned, discovered and accessed within the Catena-X Dataspace.

The standard applies to, but is not limited to, the following entities:

  • Material suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. of materials used in battery production
  • For component supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. integration, for example housing
  • Battery cells supplied to battery manufacturers

The standard shall be applied when:

  • Providing Battery Passport relevant information to data consumers.
  • Providing sustainability, compliance and traceability information required for Battery Passport generation.
  • Exchanging supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.-owned data through Catena-X Digital Twins.

The standard is not intended to be applied for:

  • Publishing Battery Passports.
  • Publishing Digital Product Passports.
  • Exchange with non-Catena-X ecosystems.
  • Provisioning of near complete battery passport information.
  • Dynamic product condition information occurring after handover to the battery manufacturer.
  • For non‑battery product passports or use cases outside the battery domain (see CX-0143)
  • For full regulatory-compliance e.g. for providing public information without access-restrictions. The standard may enable it, but as Catena-X is an ecosystem with a focus on trust and identification this is out of scope for the actual specification.

1.2 CONTEXT AND ARCHITECTURE FIT​

This section is non-normative

Information exchanged under this standard represents supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. contributions and does not necessarily correspond directly to the final Battery Passport values published by the battery manufacturer. battery manufacturers may:

  • Aggregate supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. data.
  • Enrich information with additional data sources.
  • Perform Battery Passport specific calculations.
  • Add missing information.
  • Recalculate sustainability metrics where required.

This standard therefore defines the supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. contribution layer within the Battery Passport architecture.

The standard is not intended to include all potentially relevant information for every downstream use case and also not focusing on the provisioning to non-Catena-X members. Instead, it establishes the core semantic, structural, and architectural foundation on which further domain‑specific or proprietary data products can be built. 2 Roles, Responsibilities and Process Description​

This chapter specifies the process for providing Battery Passport-relevant data from a Data Provider to a Data Consumer within the Catena-X Dataspace.

2.1 Data Provider​

A Data Provider is any actor in the battery value chain that makes Battery Passport-relevant data available to another actor. The Data Provider may be an upstream supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods., a component manufacturer, a material producer, or any other organization providing relevant information. The Data Provider:

  • MUST provide all mandatory information applicable to its supplied product.
  • MUST ensure accuracy and completeness of provided information.
  • SHOULD update information when significant product changes occur.
  • MAY provide optional information when contractually agreed.

2.2 Data Consumer​

A Data Consumer is any actor in the battery value chain that receives Battery Passport-relevant data from another actor and uses it for passport generation, compliance, or related business processes. The Data Consumer may also act as a Data Provider in other exchanges.

The Data Consumer:

  • MUST retrieve information through Catena-X compatible interfaces (Catena-X Standards which need to be followed, like CX-0002, CX-0018, CX-0151, CX-0152).

2.3 PROCESS REPRESENTATION​

This process applies where Battery Passport-relevant information originates from a supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. and is required by another actor in the battery value chain for the creation, maintenance, or provision of Battery Passport information.

The Data Provider makes relevant data available to the Data Consumer using the semantic models and exchange mechanisms referenced by this standard. The Data Consumer retrieves and processes the provided information and may combine it with information obtained from other Data Providers and from its own systems.

3 PRINCIPLES FOR DATA OBLIGATION DEFINITION​

This section is normative

SupplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. obligations differ from battery manufacturer obligations because suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. are not responsible for publishing a Battery Passport. Data obligations SHALL therefore be determined based on "SupplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.'s role" in the value chain.

3.1 Criterion 1: Product Applicability​

A data element is mandatory only when applicable to the supplied product.

Examples (include but not limited to):

  • Battery Chemistry Information is relevant only for Cell Producers, as a battery chemistry name cannot be defined for individual chemical products or components supplied to a battery.
  • Location of Hazardous Substances is applicable only to Components and Cell Producers, and not to chemical products, as it is not possible to define where within a chemical product a hazardous substance is located.

3.2 Criterion 2: Role in the Value Chain​

Data submission obligations SHALL vary depending on the role of the Data Provider within the battery value chain and the nature of the information held by the respective Data Provider.

The applicable data requirements, including mandatory and optional information, are defined according to the relevant Data Provider role and the applicable submodel. Data providers MUST provide the information applicable to their respective role as specified by Appendix A – Data Submission Obligations based on Role in the Value Chain. Optional information MAY be provided where available and where agreed between the Data Provider and Data Consumer.

3.3 Criterion 3: Data Ownership​

Information SHALL only be mandatory when the supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods. is the authoritative source of the information. Examples of supplierSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.-owned information:

  • Product Carbon Footprint
  • Material composition
  • Renewable content
  • Compliance documents

Examples of information not owned by suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.:

  • Battery-level assessments
  • Vehicle-specific information
  • Post-production product condition information

4 SEMANTIC MODELS​

This section is normative

Required Models​

This standard defines six submodels for the provisioning of Battery Passport relevant data by suppliersSupplier In the context of OSim, the producer of goods.: Digital Nameplate, Handover Documentation, Product Carbon Footprint, Circularity, Material Composition and technical data.

The Digital Nameplate data model MUST be provided together with one or more optional submodels. The selection of the applicable optional submodel(s) SHALL be agreed bilaterally between the Data Provider and the Data Consumer. The Digital Nameplate is intended to provide a common, industry-independent information basis for the identification and description of an assetAsset On the Data Provider side, an Asset describes the data set which will be shared or can be consumed by a Data Consumer.. This approach enables the Digital Nameplate information to be used within the IDTA framework, where no industry-specific core information model comparable to the Catena-X industry core is defined.

The obligation levels for each submodel defined in Sections 3 (Mandatory or Optional) refer to attributes within a submodel. They apply only once the exchange of the respective submodel has been agreed between the parties. An attribute classified as mandatory within an optional submodel is therefore only mandatory if that submodel is provided.

All data models Handover Documentation, Technical Data, Material Composition, Circularity, Carbon Footprint for Battery Passport MAY be provided, and subject to bilateral discussions.

If provided, the following semantic models MUST be provided on Type Level:

  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 2: Handover Documentation (IDTA-02035-2)
  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 4: Technical Data
  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 6: Material Composition
  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 7: Circularity

If provided, the following semantic models MUST be provided on Instance Level:

  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 1: Digital Nameplate
  • Digital Battery Passport - Part 3: Carbon Footprint for Battery Passport
